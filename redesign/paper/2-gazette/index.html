Local Learning Scientist Still Convinced Every Classroom Can Be Better

Eight years, three classrooms-worth of careers and one Master's degree later, Treta Sharma is designing learning that makes students and teachers want to show up.

By the Gazette StaffFiled from Mountain View, Calif.

For more than eight years, Treta Sharma has done the patient, stubborn work of making learning better: first by standing in front of a classroom, then by coaching the people who stand in front of many, and finally by designing the systems that reach more than a million learners at once.

She began as a Teach For India Fellow, teaching more than 120 first-generation learners in low-income schools and sitting on a School Management Committee alongside local MLAs to argue for what her school needed. She stayed on as a Fellow Advisor, training new teachers through classroom observation and long one-on-one debriefs.

At the Shiv Nadar Foundation she brought art into STEM for 450+ learners. At LEAD, an EdTech unicorn, she led curriculum analytics for 1.2 million learners across 3,000+ schools, and folded the requirements of five education boards into a single platform.

Then, in a twist her family still teases her about, she went back to school, enrolling in Northwestern's Learning Sciences programme "at the brain age of 32!" She wanted, she wrote, to become a refined distributor of knowledge rather than just a consumer of it.

Today she works at the meeting point of learning science, AI and product design, building learning products and environments that leave learners and educators "genuinely excited to show up."
