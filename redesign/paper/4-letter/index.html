Dear reader,

Thank you for stopping by. I’m a learning scientist, and for more than eight years I’ve been designing the classrooms, programs and products where people learn — always trying to make them the kind of places that leave learners and educators genuinely excited to show up.

It began in a classroom. One afternoon, one of my twelve-year-old students told me, very kindly, that we only use ten percent of our brains, and that the only person who ever used all of his was Einstein. That evening I went looking for the truth. The next morning I handed him a neuroscientist’s article that I had retitled You Can Be Smarter Than Albert Einstein. I’ve been quietly debunking small, limiting beliefs about learning ever since.

Since then I’ve taught first-generation learners and coached new teachers with Teach For India, built art-integrated STEM programs at the Shiv Nadar Foundation, and, at LEAD, worked alongside product and engineering teams on learning experiences that reached more than 1.2 million learners in over 3,000 schools.

Then, at the brain age of 32!, I went back to school for a Master’s in Learning Sciences at Northwestern. My family teased me for picking up textbooks at the age most people pick up babies. I’d do it again: I wanted to stop simply consuming knowledge and become a more careful giver of it.

These days I think a lot about AI, and about who might be left behind by it. I’ve taught both sets of our parents to use ChatGPT (the winning argument was birthday wishes for every relative), and I’m designing an AI-integrated platform for people who learn on their own.

If you’re building something that helps people learn, or simply want to talk about brains (blow a kiss to yours!), I would love to hear from you. A few enclosures follow.

Warmly,

Treta
